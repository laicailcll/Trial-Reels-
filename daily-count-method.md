# Compare your own Trial Reels posting workload

Use [daily-count-log.csv](daily-count-log.csv) to compare posting volumes on your own account without copying a universal daily target. It contains empty input rows, not example performance data.

## Fill the record

1. Enter every trial from the posting day, including weaker posts. Keep its publication reference, posting position and that day's total.
2. Mark whether the position belongs to your existing schedule or is an additional slot. Do not silently replace an existing planned post with an extra trial.
3. Choose an observation age and use it consistently. Record the actual check time and the post's age. Copy Views exactly as displayed; keep an unavailable reading blank and explain it in Notes.
4. Record the sharing state. Keep readings taken before and after wider sharing distinguishable.
5. Match each earlier posting position with a comparable baseline post. Note topic and format differences. Inspect added slots separately from existing slots.
6. Compare the batch both with and without its strongest post. If the apparent gain disappears, keep the posting count unchanged while collecting another comparable day.
7. Record creation and review time for the extra slots. Note missed planned posts or other scheduling costs. Decide whether to hold, reduce or test a larger workload, and write the observation behind that decision.

## Optional calculations

- Added-slot share = combined Views of added slots / combined Views of the day's batch. Do not calculate when the batch total is zero or the readings are incomplete.
- Added-slot Views per work minute = combined Views of added slots / recorded creation and review minutes for those slots. Do not calculate with missing time or a zero denominator. This is a workload comparison, not an Instagram metric.

Keep post ages and sharing states comparable before using either calculation. An increase does not isolate a cause. These calculations provide no pass mark or platform posting allowance.

## Limits

This is an editorial worksheet, not a controlled experiment or an Instagram policy. A publishing warning is an observed constraint to record, not a threshold to override. The sheet contains no account data and makes no claim about unique reach, non-follower reach, or future growth.

The accompanying explanation is [How Many Trial Reels Should I Post a Day?](https://trialreelsguide.com/how-many-trial-reels-should-i-post-a-day/).
